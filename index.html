Mediterranean Muscle Plan

Six weeks, two cooking sessions each. Every session (Batch A / Batch B) feeds five meals for two people, so the week runs on two pots, not eight.

How this works. Every week has the same shape. Sunday you cook Batch A — one protein, one carb, one or two vegetables — enough for Sunday dinner through Tuesday. Wednesday you cook Batch B the same way, covering Wednesday dinner through Friday. Saturday is a flex day: leftovers, a quick pantry meal, or eating out. Breakfast is your sweet potato + eggs by default, with oats or a smoothie swapped in a couple of times a week. This keeps each prep day to one protein + one pot of carbs + a tray of vegetables — not eight separate dishes.

Iron. You're taking an iron supplement, and this plan layers food-level iron on top of that: beef, beef chops, black beans and chickpeas carry most of it (ground turkey is a much leaner iron source by comparison, so those weeks lean a little more on the vitamin-C pairing). Plant and red-meat iron absorbs better next to vitamin C, so pomegranate, orange, kiwi or a squeeze of vinegar-dressed cabbage rides along with those meals on purpose. Coffee, tea and calcium-rich foods (kefir, yoghurt, cottage cheese) block iron absorption if eaten in the same sitting — keep those as separate snacks rather than side dishes to the iron-heavy meals, and it's worth taking the supplement itself away from coffee/tea and dairy for the same reason. Keep checking iron levels periodically with your doctor since food plus supplement together can add up.
Vitamin D. You're supplementing vitamin D as well. Salmon and egg yolks are the main food sources in this pantry now, so salmon is spaced out across the cycle (Week 2, then Week 6) rather than clustered, as backup on top of the supplement. Ten to fifteen minutes of midday sun most days helps too.
Cholesterol. No liver or organ meat in this cycle. Ground beef and beef chops are spread across the six weeks rather than stacked, and ground turkey covers two more red-meat-style weeks at a leaner profile — trim visible fat off the chops where you can, and choose the leanest ground beef and turkey available. About half the batches lean on fish, tofu, chickpeas or beans instead of red meat. Oats, chia, ground flax and beans bring soluble fibre, and the nuts, avocado and olive-based cooking give you unsaturated fat in place of saturated fat where it's easy to swap.
Rough protein target. For building muscle at 3–4 training days a week, most people do well aiming somewhere around 0.7–1g of protein per pound of bodyweight per day, spread across meals. This plan's lunches and dinners are built to land in the 35–45g protein range each; breakfast and snacks add the rest. Adjust portion sizes up or down against your own weight and appetite.
OMAD days (iron, vitamin D, cholesterol). With everything landing in one sitting, order matters more than usual: eat the protein-and-vegetable plate first, and save any dairy (yoghurt, kefir, cottage cheese) for a bit later in your eating window rather than on the same plate as the iron-rich food, so it doesn't blunt absorption as much. Take your iron and vitamin D supplements with the meal — vitamin D absorbs better with the fat that's already on the plate, and iron alongside the fruit course gets the vitamin-C boost. Liver still appears only once across the 11 days given the cholesterol watch. One meal a day can be harder on energy and focus with a diagnosed iron deficiency specifically, so it's worth keeping an eye on how you feel and mentioning the fasting stretch to your doctor if you haven't already.
Batch sizing. Each recipe below is written to yield about 8 servings — enough for dinner the night it's cooked plus four more lunch/dinner servings across the following two days for two people. Scale up slightly if your workouts are heavy or portions run large.
